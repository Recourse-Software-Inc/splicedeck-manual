Welcome

Thank you for choosing SpliceDeck for your LUNA recording setup.

When you record, the best ideas come quickly and leave just as quickly. SpliceDeck for LUNA keeps your hands on a Stream Deck + instead of hunting for the mouse: record, loop a passage, split and fade a clip, drop a coloured marker, balance a track, open a plug-in parameter or ride a fader into automation, all from eight keys and four dials.

SpliceDeck talks to LUNA directly, through the local servers LUNA itself runs on your Mac. It never pretends to be a keyboard, so it doesn't matter which window is in front or how you have set up your shortcuts. And because it reads LUNA all the time, every key and dial shows what is really going on: the bars and beats under the playhead, the selected track and its level, the plug-in in each slot, your markers and the Main Output meter.

The deck comes with a ready-made profile: a page for each LUNA view (LUNA Play, Timeline and Mixer) that follows LUNA as you switch views, plus a Session page and an Automation page one press away.

This manual explains how to install SpliceDeck for LUNA, how the pages are laid out and what every key and dial does, including all 38 actions. Start with Getting Started to be recording within a few minutes, then read the chapter for the page you use most.

We hope SpliceDeck becomes the part of your studio you stop noticing because it simply keeps up with you. Enjoy your session!

The SpliceDeck team at Recourse Software Inc.

Getting Started

SpliceDeck for LUNA is quick to set up. You install one plugin, open a session in LUNA, and the deck starts showing live information straight away. There is nothing to switch on in LUNA and no macOS permission to grant.

This section shows you what you need, how to install SpliceDeck for LUNA and its profile, and how to make your first moves with it.

What You Need

ItemRequirement
Stream Deck hardwareA Stream Deck +. The bundled profile is laid out for its eight keys, four dials and touch strip. The keys also work on other Stream Deck models, but the dials, and so the Jog, need a Stream Deck +.
Stream Deck softwareElgato Stream Deck app 6.9 or later.
ComputerA Mac with macOS 12 or later.
LUNAUniversal Audio LUNA 3.1, with a session open. SpliceDeck for LUNA was built and tested with LUNA 3.1.0.
Optional: ApolloFor the Monitor dial: a Universal Audio Apollo interface with UA Console installed. SpliceDeck reaches the monitor level through UA Mixer Engine, the service behind UA Console. Everything else works without an Apollo.

TipSpliceDeck for LUNA reads your keyboard's Shift and Control keys for some dial gestures. It reads them from macOS directly, so you don't need to grant Accessibility or any other permission.

Getting Started5

Installing the Plugin

  1. Double-click the installer file, for example SpliceDeck-for-LUNA-1.0.0.streamDeckPlugin. The Stream Deck app opens and asks to install SpliceDeck for LUNA. Click Install.

  2. In the action list on the right of the Stream Deck app, a new category called SpliceDeck for LUNA appears, with all 38 actions.

  3. The app also installs the SpliceDeck for LUNA profile for your Stream Deck +: five ready-made pages for LUNA. Select it in the profile menu at the top of the Stream Deck app if it isn't selected already.

  4. Start LUNA and open a session. The keys and dials come to life within a second or two.

The SpliceDeck for LUNA Profile

The profile has five pages. Three of them match LUNA's three main views, in the order of LUNA's own view selector: LUNA Play, Timeline and Mixer. Two more pages hold the things you need less often: the Session page (markers, saving, versions and export) and the Automation page (riding a track's controls).

The Timeline page of the profile. Eight keys at the top, the touch strip in the middle and four dials below it. Dial 1 is the Jog on every page.

Getting Started6

The deck follows LUNA. Whenever LUNA changes view, whether you click LUNA's view selector, use a keyboard shortcut or press the View key on the deck, the Stream Deck + switches to the matching page. SpliceDeck only ever switches pages within its own profile, never in one of yours.

The profile opens on the Timeline page. Just after the plugin starts, the deck waits for the first view change before it starts following, so if the deck and LUNA don't match at first, press the View key or change the view in LUNA once.

Getting a New Layout After an Update

The Stream Deck app adds a plugin's bundled profile only when the plugin is installed for the first time. Installing a newer version of SpliceDeck for LUNA over an older one updates the actions, but keeps the profile you already have. When a new version comes with a changed layout, install it like this:

  1. In the Stream Deck app, open the profile menu, select the SpliceDeck for LUNA profile and delete it.

  2. Uninstall the SpliceDeck for LUNA plugin from the Stream Deck app's plugin list.

  3. Double-click the new installer file. The plugin is installed fresh, and the new profile comes with it.

ImportantDeleting the profile also deletes any changes you made to it. If you have changed keys in the bundled profile, note them down first, or keep your own layout in a profile with a different name.

Your First Minutes

With LUNA open on a session and the deck on the Timeline page, try this:

Getting Started7

  1. Turn the Jog (dial 1). The playhead moves a 1/16 note per click and the counter on the strip follows as you turn. Spin it quickly to move by beats or bars. When you stop, LUNA's Timeline centres on the playhead.

  2. Turn the Track dial (dial 4) to select a track. The strip shows its name, its R I S M buttons and its level. Tap S on the strip to solo it, tap again to unsolo.

  3. Park the Jog in the middle of a clip and push it. The clips at the playhead are split. Press Undo to take it back.

  4. Hold Shift on your keyboard and turn the Jog near the start of a clip. A fade-in appears and grows as you turn. Turn back to zero for no fade.

  5. Press Play, then press View. LUNA switches to the Mixer and the deck follows. Hold View for half a second to reach the Session page, and press it there to come back.

Understanding SpliceDeck for LUNA

This section explains how SpliceDeck for LUNA connects to LUNA, how to read its keys and touch strip, how the five pages fit together and how the keyboard's Shift and Control keys change what the dials do.

How It Works

While LUNA is running, it keeps a few small servers open on your Mac: one for its user interface (commands, views, the transport and the counter) and one for the session itself (tracks, clips, fades, markers, the selection, the loop, tempo, key and plug-in controls). LUNA Play uses the same session server. SpliceDeck for LUNA connects to both and talks to LUNA directly.

  • No keystroke macros. SpliceDeck doesn't type shortcuts into LUNA. Your keyboard layout and custom shortcuts don't matter, and the deck works whichever window is in front.
  • Nothing to switch on. You don't need to enable anything in LUNA or grant any macOS permission.
  • Everything stays on your Mac. SpliceDeck connects only to LUNA on your own computer, and to UA Mixer Engine for the Monitor dial. It doesn't connect to the internet.
  • Live in both directions. SpliceDeck reads LUNA many times a second, so keys and dials also follow what you do with the mouse or keyboard in LUNA.

NoteThe interface SpliceDeck uses is internal to LUNA and not documented by Universal Audio. SpliceDeck is careful with it: when something it expects isn't there, the key concerned shows that it can't work rather than guessing. A future LUNA update may change this interface; check for a matching SpliceDeck update if keys stop working after you update LUNA.

Reading a Key

Every key is drawn live from LUNA's current state. A key shows its symbol, its name and, on many keys, a small live value underneath: the bar and beat on Play, the selected send and its level on Sends, the plug-in in the selected slot on Inserts.

Understanding SpliceDeck9

Live value. The Sends key while it is held: send 2, at −10.0 dB.

Lit. Loop is lit while LUNA loops.

Recording. Record turns red and shows the selected track's input level.

  • Lit keys. A key lights up while what it stands for is on: Play while LUNA plays (green), Record while it records (red), Loop, Click and Count-In while they are on, a Mixer element key while you hold it.
  • Dimmed keys. A key dims when it has nothing to do: Undo and Redo when there is nothing to undo or redo, Sends when the selected send is off, Inserts when the plug-in in the slot is bypassed.
  • Confirmations. After a press, some keys briefly show a short green confirmation, such as Saved, Marker added or Split.
  • Errors. When LUNA can't do what a key asks, the key flashes a short red message for about a second, for example No clip here or Not available, and then returns to normal.

Reading the Touch Strip

The touch strip above the dials is split into four parts, one per dial. Each part shows the dial's name at the top left, its current value in large type, and a short hint or second value underneath.

The Track dial's part of the strip. Top: the page colour band. Top left: the group code and the name (TRACK). Top right: LUNA's current view. Centre: the selected track. Below: LUNA's R I S M buttons, lit while on, and the selected track's level. Along the bottom: the Main Output meter.

Understanding SpliceDeck10

The page colour band

A thin band of colour runs across the top of the whole touch strip. Its colour is the colour of LUNA's current view: teal for LUNA Play, orchid for the Timeline and azure for the Mixer. The View key (key 8 on every page) is lit in the same colour and shows the view's name, so you can always see at a glance which page you are on.

Group colours and corner codes

Every action belongs to a group, and every group has its own colour. You see it in the key's symbol and in the strip. So that you never have to rely on colour alone, each key and each part of the strip also carries a small group code in its top-left corner: a row of up to three dots and dashes. The same code appears in the footer of the chapters of this manual that are about one group.

Understanding SpliceDeck12

"Hold" on the deck always means keeping a key or dial pressed for about half a second. On the Session and Automation pages, the strip's colour band keeps showing LUNA's current view, because LUNA stays in that view; the View key tells you which page you are on. If LUNA changes view while you are on the Session or Automation page, the deck follows LUNA to that view's page.

The Meters

The Track dial's strip with the selected track's level under the R I S M buttons, and the Main Output meter (two hairlines, left and right) along the bottom edge.

  • Main Output. A stereo meter, two hairlines for left and right, runs along the bottom of the whole touch strip on every page, continuous across the four dials. It uses LUNA's own meter scale: green, then amber from 75% of the scale and red from 90%, with LUNA's held peak and a red clip light at the right end.
  • Selected track. The selected track's level is shown under the R I S M buttons on the Track dial (Timeline, Mixer, Session and Automation pages) and on the Song dial while it is set to Track (LUNA Play page).
  • Record key. While LUNA records, the Record key shows the selected track's input level along its bottom.

The meters refresh about 15 times a second while there is signal, and 4 times a second while LUNA is silent.

Scrubbing

The Jog on the Timeline page: the counter in bars | beats | ticks, a beat ruler with the playhead, and push: split at the top right. On the other pages the top right shows the tempo.

Each click of the dial moves the playhead a 1/16 note. Spin it quickly and each click moves a beat; spin it fast and each click moves a bar. That way small turns are precise and you can still cross a whole song in a moment.

The Jog Dial32

The counter on the strip updates the instant you turn, before LUNA has redrawn, so the dial always feels immediate. While LUNA plays, the counter follows the playback about 18 times a second.

The playhead moves exactly as it does with LUNA's own navigation: it moves the playback start point, so pressing Play plays from where you scrubbed to.

Keeping the playhead in view

On the Timeline, when you stop turning the Jog, LUNA's view centres on the playhead, at any window size or zoom. This happens once you stop, not continuously while you turn, because LUNA's display lags behind its session model while the playhead moves quickly.

The view isn't moved while LUNA plays, because LUNA's own Auto-Scroll follows the playhead then, or while a loop range is selected, because LUNA keeps the selected range in view itself.

Splitting and Deleting Clips

Hold the Jog to delete the clip under the playhead on the selected track. The strip confirms with Deleted and the clip's name, or shows No clip here if there is none. Undo brings the clip back.

TipTo cut out a section, scrub to its start, push, scrub to its end, push again, then park inside the section and hold.

Mixing and Automation

This chapter looks more closely at the elements of a track on the Mixer page, and at how the Automation page turns your rides into automation.

Input

Hold Input. Dial 2 is the track's input trim: about 1 dB per click, 3 dB on a quick spin; push for 0 dB. Dial 3 is the phase: any turn flips it, and the strip shows Ø or normal. When the track's input trim is linked, both sides move together. The Input key shows the trim, with Ø while the phase is flipped.

Inserts and Rec FX

Inserts are the track's eight plug-in slots. Rec FX are its four record-path plug-in slots, such as Unison preamps on an Apollo input. Both work the same way. Hold the key, then:

  1. Pick the slot with dial 2. The strip shows the plug-in in the slot, ● ON or ○ BYPASSED, and the slot number (1/8). Push dial 2 to switch the plug-in on or bypass it. An empty slot reads empty.

  2. Pick a parameter with dial 3. Turn to step through the plug-in's parameters. The strip shows the parameter's name, its value and its number, for example 5/12.

  3. Push dial 3 to edit it. The title changes to EDIT. Turn to change the value, 1% per click, 5% on a quick spin. Push again when you are done.

Dial 2. Slot 1 holds a vocal suite, switched on.

Dial 3, turn. Parameter 5: Tune Speed.

Dial 3, pushed. Editing its value.

A tap on Inserts or Rec FX, without turning a dial, bypasses the plug-in in the selected slot or switches it back on. The key shows the slot number and the plug-in's name, and dims while it is bypassed.

Mixing and Automation41

Sends and Cues

Hold Sends. Dial 2 picks the send, 1 to 8; dial 3 sets its level, about 1 dB per click. Push dial 3 to switch the send between pre fader and post fader. Cues works the same way for the track's four cue mixes.

A tap on Sends or Cues switches the selected send or cue on or off. The key shows the send's number and level, or off.

TipThe send, cue, slot and parameter you picked are remembered while you work, so the next time you hold the key, the dials pick up where you left off.

How a Ride Is Recorded

On the Automation page, each lane key stands for one control of the selected track. Hold the key and turn dial 2 to ride it.

  1. Press Play and hold a lane key, for example Volume. While you hold it, the track's automation is switched off, so what you hear is the dial, not the automation already there.

  2. Turn dial 2. While LUNA plays, the control's value at the playhead is recorded about 10 times a second and every time you turn. The key and the strip turn red with ● REC.

  3. Let go of the key. The pass is written as automation over the stretch you rode: the automation points in that stretch are replaced by your pass, using LUNA's own linear automation points. The whole pass is one undo step.

  4. Listen back. The track plays the pass back. If the track was in Off or Read, it is set to Read; in Touch, Latch or Write it stays in that mode.

If LUNA is stopped, the Ride dial simply sets the control's value; nothing is recorded. The strip shows play to rec as a reminder.

Changing the Profile

The bundled profile is an ordinary Stream Deck profile. Select any key or dial and drag a different action onto it, or change its settings, as with any profile. Keep in mind:

  • Page order matters. SpliceDeck switches to the pages of the SpliceDeck for LUNA profile by position: LUNA Play, Timeline, Mixer, Session, Automation. Keep the five pages in this order.

Common Problems

The profile doesn't appear, or has the old layout

The Stream Deck app adds the bundled profile only on a fresh install, and only for a Stream Deck +. If it is missing, or still shows an older layout after an update, delete the SpliceDeck for LUNA profile, uninstall the plugin, then install it again, as described in Getting a New Layout After an Update.

The deck doesn't follow LUNA's view

  • Check that the SpliceDeck for LUNA profile is the one selected for your Stream Deck +. SpliceDeck switches pages only in its own profile.
  • Just after SpliceDeck starts, it waits for LUNA's first view change. Change the view once, or press View.
  • Check that the five pages are still in their original order.

The Monitor dial shows "no Apollo monitor"

The Monitor dial needs a UA Apollo and UA Console's mixer engine (UA Mixer Engine) running on your Mac. Check that the Apollo is connected and UA Console works. Without an Apollo, use Shift and Control on dial 2 for track volume and pan; everything else works normally.

Keys look dim

A dim key usually means there is nothing for it to do right now: Undo with nothing to undo, a send that is off, a bypassed plug-in, or LUNA Status with no session open. It isn't a fault.

Help49

Shift or Control gestures don't work

  • Hold the key on your Mac's keyboard before you turn the dial, and keep it held while you turn.
  • Shift + Jog and Shift + Track work while LUNA shows its Timeline view.
  • For a fade, the selected track must have a clip edge near the playhead. For a selection, start in a gap on the selected track.

The view jumps or doesn't centre

The Timeline centres on the playhead when you stop turning the Jog, not while you turn. It doesn't centre while LUNA plays (LUNA's Auto-Scroll follows then) or while a loop range is selected (LUNA keeps the range in view).

Something changed in LUNA but the deck shows the old value

Give it a moment: SpliceDeck reads LUNA many times a second. If the deck stays out of step, press the LUNA Status key (if you have one) to reconnect, or quit and reopen the Stream Deck app.

Known Limits

LUNA has no public interface for controllers like SpliceDeck, so a few things work differently from what you might expect, or aren't possible yet.

  • LUNA redraws outside changes about 10 times a second. When you turn a dial fast, LUNA's own display can lag a little behind the deck. The deck shows the new value at once.
  • The view centres when you stop turning. The Timeline doesn't scroll continuously with the Jog; it centres on the playhead once you stop.
  • A fade rolled back to zero leaves an empty fade. The fade is silent, but LUNA keeps an empty fade object on the clip, because LUNA's Delete Fades command doesn't act on a fade picked from outside LUNA.
  • Playback speed isn't reported. LUNA doesn't report the playback speed, so the Speed dial's strip shows the last speed set from the deck.
  • Names are typed in a separate box. LUNA's own name field can't take the keyboard from outside, so markers are named in SpliceDeck's small macOS box.
  • macOS and LUNA 3.1. SpliceDeck for LUNA runs on macOS only and has been tested with LUNA 3.1. A later LUNA version may change the interface it uses.

Your Data

SpliceDeck for LUNA runs entirely on your Mac. It talks to LUNA through LUNA's local servers, and to UA Mixer Engine for the Monitor dial, and doesn't connect to the internet. Your key settings and SpliceDeck's settings are stored by the Stream Deck app.
